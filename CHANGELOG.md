## 0.3.4 — unreleased

### Added

- **Protected-plus-permitted left turns in the Chapter 23 interchange pipeline** (`ProtectedPermittedLeft` on `LaneGroupInput`, `protected_sat_flow` and `permitted_sat_flow` on `LaneGroupResult`). Such a movement is one lane group with two phase components rather than two lane groups, and Exhibit 34-78 is what settles that: it carries a single saturation flow, effective green, capacity and v/c for each arterial left turn of the Example Problem 7 SPUI, and Steps 6, 7 and 9 read only those. The two components therefore survive in exactly two places. In Step 3 the protected component keeps its Equation 23-20 radius factor while the permitted component takes the Equation 31-100 gap-acceptance rate through the rest of the Equation 23-14 chain (Equation 31-110), and the two capacities add, so the single saturation flow the exhibit reports is that sum re-expressed over the summed green: `(1,560 × 16 + 561 × 13.01) / 48 = 672` eastbound and `(1,561 × 16 + 573 × 11.78) / 48 = 661` westbound reproduce the two published values exactly. In Step 8 the uniform delay comes from the Chapter 31 Exhibit 31-15 queue accumulation polygon instead of Equation 19-19, which assumes one service period per cycle; driving `qap_evaluate` with the published Exhibit 34-75 component saturation flows reproduces the published 22.1 and 22.8 s/veh. Steps 4, 6, 7 and 9 needed no change at all. The field is `serde`-defaulted, so every existing fixture deserializes unchanged and no asserted number moves anywhere — verified by dumping every f64 the pipeline produces for all six existing fixtures as raw IEEE-754 bits before and after and comparing them byte for byte (56,559 bytes, identical, md5 `c5e5c525…`; the same dump under a 1.0000001 scaling of each fixture's cycle length differs at byte 43, so the comparison is not vacuous).

- **Chapter 34 Example Problem 7 (single-point urban interchange) as `tests/ExampleCases/hcm/RampTerminals/case7.json`.** This is the first SPUI fixture, and it confirms the single-point convention that was already in the routing: `turning_movements_from_od` sends each of the ten O-Ds through exactly one lane group, an `Ext` group for the arterial approaches and a `Ramp` group for the off-ramps, so `od_path` returns a one-element path and the O-D delay is the movement delay unchanged. That is what Exhibit 34-82 shows, its ETT column being the Exhibit 34-80 / 34-81 movement delays with no extra distance traveled. Eight of the ten published O-D LOS letters and the interchange LOS reproduce, and the interchange reads 45.4 s/veh against the published 48.3. The two letters that differ, O-Ds D and E, are the two nearest an Exhibit 23-10 boundary.

- **The westbound external left queue storage ratio of Chapter 34 Example Problem 2 is pinned at its published value.** The test asserted the eastbound external left at the published R_Q of 1.96, which is the movement that overflows its 200 ft bay, and left the westbound one merely bounded below 1.0. ### Known issues

- **The Example Problem 7 saturation flow exhibits are not reproducible from the HCM 7 equations, and three separate defects account for it.** Every one of the fourteen lane group columns of Exhibits 34-75 and 34-76 lists a lane width adjustment of 0.967 for the stated 10.3-ft lanes, where HCM 7 Exhibit 19-20 is a three-tier lookup returning 1.000 for every width from 10.0 to 12.9 ft; 0.967 is the superseded HCM 2000 continuous form `1 + (W − 12)/30` evaluated at 11 ft, so the exhibits were carried over from an earlier edition. Exhibit 34-76 then lists a heavy-vehicle factor of 1.000 for the northbound and southbound approaches, which the example's own text gives 5% heavy vehicles and which Equation 19-10 puts at 0.961 — the same 0.961 the exhibit prints for the eastbound and westbound approaches at 3.4% heavy vehicles on a 2% grade. The two deviations nearly cancel on the ramp approaches, which is why their delays still land within 0.5 s/veh while the arterial ones do not. Finally the traffic pressure row reproduces exactly from Equation 23-15 for all ten columns that are not a left-turn phase component and for none of the four that are: the equation gives 0.967 eastbound and 0.971 westbound against a published 0.950 / 0.951 and 0.950 / 0.954, and the published protected and permitted values differ from each other for a single movement at a single demand, which the equation cannot do.

- **Exhibit 34-77 contradicts itself on the westbound left turn, and Exhibit 34-80 contradicts it on the same movement's uniform delay.** The exhibit's own relation `g_u = G_perm − g_q − l_1` closes eastbound (32 − 17 − 2 = 13.0 against a published 13.01) and not westbound (32 − 20 − 2 = 10.0 against a published 11.78, which instead implies g_q = 18.22). `g_u` is the live value of the pair, because it is what enters the polygon and both published values reproduce their published uniform delays, so `case7.json` carries g_u and not g_q. Separately, Exhibit 34-77 prints a westbound uniform delay of 22.7 s/veh where Exhibit 34-80 prints 22.8 for the same movement; 22.8 is the live one, since it is what Exhibit 34-80's own `d = d1 + d2 + d3 = 22.8 + 11.8 = 34.6` adds up to. The engine lands at 22.76. Exhibit 34-77 also labels its three breakpoint queues "ft"; they are vehicles, as Chapter 31 defines every polygon breakpoint queue and as the arithmetic on both columns confirms.

- **Chapter 34 omits the sneaker term that Chapter 31 requires of the same capacity.** Equation 31-124 gives a protected-plus-permitted exclusive left-turn lane group a capacity of `[g_l s_lt / C + (g_u s_l + 3,600 n_s) / C] N` with n_s = 2.0 veh clearing on the change interval each cycle. Exhibit 34-78's published 293 veh/h for the eastbound left omits it; including it would give 359. Chapter 31's own Example Problem 1 includes it. The interchange pipeline follows Chapter 34 and omits it, and `test_ep7_capacity_omits_the_sneaker_term` pins the size of that choice rather than leaving it implicit.

- **`g_u` is supplied by the fixture rather than derived.** Exhibit 31-12 builds the unblocked permitted green from the opposing queue service time, and for this SPUI that route does not close: the opposing arterial through runs at v/c 0.91 and needs about 27.9 s of its 32-s phase to serve its own queue, which would leave roughly 2 s rather than the 13.01 s Exhibit 34-77 publishes. Both published values do reproduce their published uniform delays through the polygon, so they are taken as the published intermediates they are, with a VERIFY-HCM note at the field.

## 0.3.3 — 2026-08

### Added

- **Chapter 23 lane groups are composed rather than enumerated, which is what the parclo family needed.** `InterchangeMovement` was a closed ten-variant enum shaped by the diamond skeleton, and every one of its variants was already spoken for, so there was no way to name an external left onto a parclo A loop, an internal shared through-and-right, or an exclusive external right turn. It is now a composition of an `InterchangeApproach`, a `MovementPosition` (`External`/`Internal`/`Ramp`/`RampTwo`), and a `MovementTurn` (`Left`/`Through`/`Right`/`ThroughRight`), serialised as the concatenation of the three names. The ten diamond movements are the compositions `EbExtThrough` through `SbRampRight`, which is exactly what the closed enum serialised, so every fixture and every Python caller is unaffected — there are no aliases and no compatibility shim, because the legacy names *are* compositions. `Debug` and `Display` print the same string. The `movements` module exposes each composition as a constant for `match` arms. Every asserted number in every Chapter 23 test is unchanged by the refactor, verified by dumping every f64 the pipeline produces for all five existing fixtures as raw IEEE-754 bits before and after and comparing them byte for byte (22,271 bytes, identical; a 0.0001 perturbation of one fixture's peak hour factor moves 44 lines of the same dump, so the comparison is not vacuous).

- **Routing and lane group demand composition are derived from the Chapter 34 worksheets instead of being tabulated per form.** `od_journey(form, m)` pushes a unit demand on an O-D through `turning_movements_from_od` and reads back the turning movements it lands in; `Interchange::resolve_movement` maps each of those onto a lane group the interchange actually has, folding an internal right into an internal through-and-right group and an external turn into the external through group when the direction shares. The worksheets already encode which turn each O-D makes at each terminal — that O-D F is an external right in a diamond and an external left onto the loop in a parclo A-2Q, that a parclo A internal approach turns right onto the freeway where a diamond turns left — so nothing has to say it a second time, and all nine Exhibit 23-17 forms route without a per-form table. Two new tests run every form end to end on a synthetic configuration and assert that every O-D carrying demand reaches at least one lane group.

- **Chapter 34 Example Problem 2 (Parclo A-2Q) as `tests/ExampleCases/hcm/RampTerminals/case6.json`.** This is the first fixture that is not a diamond skeleton. Its arterial approaches are an external through, an external left onto the loop, and an internal shared through-and-right in each direction. Every Exhibit 34-29 O-D level of service letter and both O-D flags reproduce, ETTs within 0.8 s/veh, and the interchange reads 61.5 s/veh and LOS D against a published 61.3 and D. Effective greens are exact on all ten lane groups, and capacities land within 2 veh/h of Exhibits 34-25 and 34-26 on the eight that are not an internal through-and-right. Three things about the example are documented rather than matched, each with the evidence in `docs/hcm/VERIFICATION.md`: Exhibit 34-25's printed demand of 1,282 veh/h for the eastbound internal group against the worksheet's 1,356 (Exhibit 34-27's own v/c of 0.56 and Exhibit 34-25's own arrival rate of 0.38 veh/s both give 1,356); Exhibit 34-22's lane utilization factor of 1.000 on the internal groups against Chapter 19's 0.908, which Examples 1, 3, and 4 all print and which produces a better O-D table here than the 1.000 does; and Exhibit 34-20's rightmost-lane utilization shares, which need an Exhibit 23-24 coefficient of 0.655 where the exhibit prints 0.605.

- **`ExtraDistance::design_speed_mph`**, a per-movement override of the interchange-wide extra-distance design speed. Equation 23-50 defines v_D per diverted movement and Example Problem 2 mixes two of them, evaluating its loop-ramp O-Ds at 25 mi/h and the rest at 35. The field defaults to `None`, which keeps the interchange-wide value, so no existing fixture moves.

### Fixed

- **Exclusive external right-turn lane groups are analyzed instead of being scored as free-flowing.** Chapter 34 Example Problem 3 has exclusive eastbound and westbound external right-turn lanes, which the ten-variant movement enum could not name, so `case3.json` carried the ten groups it could name and left O-Ds F and G with an empty path. Step 9 then scored them as free-flowing at -1.2 s/veh against a published 19.1 and 27.9. The fixture now carries `EbExtRight` and `WbExtRight` groups and the two O-Ds route through them. Published against engine: saturation flow 1,675 / 1,674.998 and 1,614 / 1,613.69 veh/h, capacity 824 / 823.54 and 524 / 524.45 veh/h, control delay 20.3 / 20.32 and 29.1 / 29.11 s/veh, queue storage ratio 0.23 / 0.227 and 0.06 / 0.062. O-D F lands at 19.16 s/veh and O-D G at 27.94, both LOS B as published, and the interchange ETT moves from 108.3 to 110.24 against the published 110.3. Nothing else in Example Problem 3 moves: a byte-for-byte comparison of the whole pipeline dump before and after shows the two new lane groups, O-Ds F and G, and the interchange ETT, and no other line in any fixture.

## 0.3.2 — 2026-08

### Added

- **HCM Chapter 20, Section 5 two-way STOP pedestrian mode** (`src/hcm/twsc/pedestrian.rs`, PyO3 binding `analyze_twsc_pedestrian`). This is the procedure where the pedestrian is the subject and the service measure is the proportion of pedestrians dissatisfied with the crossing, distinct from the Section 4 pedestrian impedance already in `twsc.rs` that reduces vehicular capacity. Covers the two-stage crossing decomposition, group critical headway with the optional platoon adjustment, blocked-lane and delayed-crossing probabilities, gap delay, the motorist-yield reduction, and the satisfaction model. Reproduces every published value of Chapter 32 TWSC Example Problem 2 across its three scenarios. Two things the source text does not settle are marked VERIFY-HCM in the module: the Equation 20-95 I_MR and I_NY coefficients, which the PDF clips and which were solved out of the six published O(S/D) values that over-determine them, and which stage's P_d and P(Y_1) feed Equations 20-98 and 20-99 on a two-stage crossing, where the first stage is used.

### Fixed

- **The Equation 25-12 front-clearing test is scoped to a bottleneck whose own capacity was restored.** The predicate was implemented as the printed inequality, `[SC - ONRD]` rising and exceeding `SD`, which fires on any period where on-ramp demand falls, on any segment, whether or not it has a queue of its own to clear. The prose introducing MO3 does not read that way: it presents the constraint as coming from "downstream queues clearing from their downstream end", queues "typically caused by incidents in which there is a temporary reduction in capacity". `OversaturatedEngine::front_clearing_active` now adds the two conditions that carry that scope, the segment being its own active bottleneck in the preceding period and its own capacity being restored, and `analyze_oversaturated` calls it instead of the bare inequality. `front_clearing_queue` is unchanged and still returns Equation 25-12 exactly as printed. Chapter 25 Example Problems 2 and 4 both have on-ramp demand falling on Segments 2, 6 and 8 in analysis periods 4 and 5, so both were arming MO3 on segments queued from a bottleneck elsewhere and starving them through a wave-travel-time lookback into the congested period. Example Problem 4 mean absolute error against the published matrices falls from 1.23 to 0.91 mi/h on speed, 7.93 to 5.81 veh/mi/ln on density and 62 to 56 veh/h on volume served; its period 4 Segment 6 density moves from 69.8 to 112.9 against a published 117.3 and its period 5 Segment 2 speed from 26.7 to 14.1 against a published 16.4. Example Problem 2 moves much less, because MO3 was binding near capacity there rather than far below it, but its period 4 Segment 4 LOS letter moves from D to the published E. Analysis periods 1 through 3 of both problems are bit-identical, since the predicate was false in all of them.

- **MO3 no longer imposes a stale constraint from the first time step of a recovery period.** Equation 25-13's text says "the clearing does not affect the segment throughput until the recovery wave has reached the upstream end". The wave-travel-time lookback reads history ring buffers that span earlier analysis periods, so with front clearing armed it reached back into the fully congested period and applied its throughput immediately, before any wave could have traveled anywhere. A per-segment step counter now holds the constraint until WTT steps have elapsed since clearing started, and resets when clearing ends. On the two-segment incident case in the engine's own tests this is worth 434 veh/h of recovery throughput, 4,621 against 4,187. No published example problem moves, because after the scoping fix above none of them arms front clearing at all.

- **Chapter 23 common green is scoped to a phase pair, not to a movement.** `common_green_time` unioned a movement's green windows before intersecting, so a movement served twice per cycle overstated its common green. Exhibit 23-28 draws each common green as the overlap of one upstream phase's window with one downstream phase's window, and the Step 4 text defines it "between Phase A ... and Phase B", so each window is now a separate candidate and the largest single-pair overlap governs. The eastbound internal through of Chapter 34 Example Problems 1 and 8 is the case that separates the two readings: its 150-53 and 116-150 windows are contiguous, so unioning them gave the southbound ramp the whole 39-s ramp green, where Exhibit 34-9 and Exhibit 34-89 both print CG_RD = 34 s. Example Problem 1 corroborates 34 downstream, since Exhibit 34-10's published 4.1-ft southbound-left queue comes out of Equation 23-34 at 34 s and 0 ft at 39 s. No published result moves: the only fixture with a twice-served movement is Example Problem 1, whose queue at either value leaves more than 200 ft of storage on a 500-ft internal link, so its additional lost time is zero under both. The book does not say which pair governs when more than one qualifies, and the maximum carries a VERIFY-HCM note saying so.

- **Exhibit 15-6 LOS thresholds are selected by posted speed limit, not by average travel speed.** Chapter 15 states the split as "On higher-speed two-lane highways (≥50 mi/h)" and prints the Exhibit 15-6 column headers as "Posted Speed Limit ≥ 50 mi/h" and "Posted Speed Limit < 50 mi/h". `determine_segment_los` and `determine_facility_los` already documented their `s_pl` parameter as the posted limit, and `docs/hcm/procedures/chapter15.md` already flagged it, but every caller in the repository passed the computed average speed instead: the two analysis scripts, the River Falls gate, the Rust integration and example-problem harnesses, `tests/common/mod.rs`, the README, the book, and the crate's own doc examples. No published value moves, because on all four Chapter 26 fixtures and on River Falls the posted limit and the average speed fall on the same side of 50 mi/h, so this closes a latent trap rather than correcting a result. HCM Step 11 defines no facility-level posted limit for a mixed-limit facility, so the facility callers length-weight it, which reduces to the common value when every segment shares one.
- **`case_study1.json` now carries the River Falls peak hour factor and opposing volume.** The fixture is the River Falls corridor rather than an HCM example problem, and it had `phf` 0.9 on segments 1 through 4 against 0.94 on segment 5 (one facility, one analysis period, two factors) and `volume_op` 0 throughout, against the 0.94 and the 512 veh/h opposing volume that `analyze_river_falls.py` records as verbatim from the analysis request. Opposing volume is inert on the passing-constrained segments, where the engine substitutes the 1,500 veh/h high-opposing-flow assumption, but it drives the two passing zones. The fixture's `phv` and `sup_ele` were already correct in percent and are unchanged. No Rust test moves, because `read_test_files` excludes `case_study*` by shape.
- **`run_case_study.py` no longer expects a superseded number and no longer exits zero on a mismatch.** It expected a facility follower density of 5.25, which the project superseded along with 5.09. It now pins this engine's own output for the fixture, at a 0.001 tolerance, and exits nonzero when anything drifts. Its expectations are engine output rather than published values, because the River Falls corridor has no published answer column, and the header says so.

### Known issues

- **The River Falls facility follower density is 5.223 by one construction and 5.310 by another, and the difference is a unit error in the first.** `analyze_river_falls.py` and `tests/test_river_falls_gate.py` build the facility in Python with `phv=0.08` and `sup_ele=0.02` as fractions where the engine reads percent, which understates the heavy-vehicle free-flow-speed penalty and drops both curve subsegments into a lower horizontal class. Correcting only those two, holding every other input fixed, gives 5.309; `case_study1.json` gives 5.310, the residual 0.001 being the fixture rounding segment 3 to 2.34 mi where its subsegments sum to 2.3390 mi. The two constructions otherwise converge. 5.223 is unchanged here because it is the value currently reported in the paper.

## 0.3.1 — 2026-08

### Fixed

- **Interchange-level LOS no longer carries the per-O-D v/c and R_Q flags.** Exhibit 23-10's "automatically LOS F" rule applies to an individual O-D, and each O-D traveling through a lane group with v/c > 1 or R_Q > 1 still reads F. Chapter 23 Step 9 grades the interchange from the demand-weighted experienced travel time alone, and it explicitly anticipates a failing O-D being masked at the interchange level, directing the analyst to report the poorest O-D as context rather than to force the aggregate letter down. The engine had been propagating `any_vc` / `any_rq` into the aggregate, so any interchange with one oversaturated lane group graded F regardless of its ETT. Chapter 34 Example Problem 3 now reads LOS E against its published E, and Example Problem 4 reads D against its published D; both previously read F.
- **Incremental delay d2 uses the lane group capacity, not the per-lane capacity.** Equation 19-26 defines c_A as "the average capacity (veh/h) ... equal to the capacity c computed in Step 7", and the Step 7 c is a lane group capacity. The interchange engine had been dividing by c/N, following the Chapter 34 worksheets. The change only affects multilane groups, and it moves the worked examples decisively toward their published values. Example Problem 3's eastbound external through reproduces the published d2 of 110.5 s/veh at 110.36 against 119.9 per-lane, and its interchange ETT lands at 108.3 against the published 110.3 (was 115.2). Example Problem 4's interchange ETT lands at 78.1 against the published 78.0 (was 92.4), which is most of what had been recorded as an unreproducible "external capacity defect". Example Problem 5's O-D E, which runs entirely on a 3-lane external crossover at v/c = 0.84 and so isolates this term most cleanly, reproduces the published 24.7 s/veh and LOS B exactly, where the per-lane form gave 33.9 and the wrong letter. Example Problem 1 is the one worked example that disagrees: its published d2 of 4.6 s/veh reproduces only per-lane (4.65 against 2.33 lane-group), so its worked values are inconsistent with the definition Equation 19-26 gives for its own variable. It is treated as a book defect alongside the December 2022 errata, on the grounds that the equation text and two other example problems outvote one worksheet, and the affected expectations in the Chapter 23 test suites are pinned to engine values with the published ones recorded inline. The signalized engine in `src/hcm/signalized/` already used the lane group capacity and is unchanged.
- **Two-lane facility follower density aggregates the adjusted segment densities.** Equation 15-39 defines FD_i as "follower density, or adjusted follower density, for segment i" and directs that FD_PLmid is used for passing lane segments. No aggregation existed in the library at all; every caller reweighted the unadjusted per-segment column itself, which discarded the entire Step 9 downstream passing-lane benefit that Chapter 26 Example Problem 3 spends most of its length computing. `TwoLaneHighways::determine_facility_follower_density` now implements Equation 15-39 in one place, exposed through the PyO3 bindings, and the test harnesses and analysis scripts call it. Example Problem 3's facility moves from 8.041 followers/mi and LOS D to 7.271 and LOS C, against the published 7.3 and LOS C in Exhibit 26-27. Example Problem 4 moves from 20.219 to 19.897 against the published 20.0, staying in the LOS E band that had been masking the same omission. The River Falls case study is unaffected at 5.223 followers/mi, because the Step 9 chain cannot activate on a facility with no passing lane segment, and a regression test now states that dependency explicitly rather than leaving it implicit.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-08

### Added

- **HCM Edition 7.1 support, selectable per segment.** Edition 7.1 (November 2025) replaces Chapters 13, 14, 27, and 28 with new weaving, merge, and diverge methodologies from NCHRP Research Report 1038; the rest of the 7th Edition is unchanged. `HcmVersion` (`V7`, `V7_1`) selects the edition on `WeavingSegment` and `RampSegment`, and `run_analysis()` dispatches on it. From Python: `WeavingSegment(version="7.1", ...)`, a settable `.version` property, `.analysis_v7_1()` for the full typed result, and `hcm_versions()` / `hcm_latest_version()` / `hcm_version_changes_chapter()` for building a version picker.
- **Edition 7.1 Chapter 13** (`src/hcm/weaving/v7_1.rs`): overall speed as an equivalent basic segment less a speed impedance (Eqs 13-7 through 13-14), capacity solved analytically from the 35 pc/mi/ln breakdown density (Eqs 13-15 through 13-19), the simple weaving volume estimation method (Eqs 13-2 through 13-6), and Exhibit 13-7 LOS. Reproduces Chapter 27 Example Problems 1, 2, and 3 value for value.
- **Edition 7.1 Chapter 14** (`src/hcm/merge_diverge/v7_1.rs`): merge and diverge speed impedance (Eqs 14-4/14-5), the capacity quadratic (Eqs 14-8 through 14-14), all three capacity checks (Exhibits 14-8, 14-9, 14-10), and Exhibit 14-2 LOS. Reproduces Chapter 28 Example Problems 1 and 2 value for value.
- `WeavingSegment` gains `nw_rf`, `nw_fr`, and `nw_rr`, the weaving-lane counts the Edition 7.1 configuration weighting reads. The 7th Edition methodology ignores them.

### Breaking

- **`RampSegment::determine_los` and `RampSegment::run_analysis` now return `Option<LevelOfService>`**, and the PyO3 `RampSegment.run_analysis()` returns `None` instead of a letter. A major merge operating under capacity has no 7th Edition level of service; the previous code set `self.los = None` but returned `LevelOfService::E`, so a caller reading the return value got a letter the HCM does not sanction. Under Edition 7.1 this case always yields a letter, because Exhibit 14-2 extends its criteria to major merges and diverges.
- **The Python step methods raise on a `version="7.1"` segment.** `determine_demand_flow()`, `determine_capacity()`, `estimate_speed()`, `determine_los()`, and the other stepwise methods implement the 7th Edition's numbered steps, which Edition 7.1 replaced with a different structure that has no one-to-one equivalents. They previously ran 7th Edition math silently regardless of the segment's version; they now raise `ValueError` pointing at `run_analysis()` / `analysis_v7_1()`. The two editions disagree on speeds, capacities, and LOS bands, so a silent wrong-edition number was the worst available behavior.
- **`speed_avg` in the Edition 7.1 analysis results is optional.** When demand is so far past capacity that the speed impedance consumes the whole equivalent-basic-segment speed, Equations 13-7 and 14-2/14-3 have no physical meaning; the serialized `speed_avg` is now `null` there instead of a negative number. Density is infinite and LOS reads F in that regime, as before.
- **`BasicFreeways::lc_r` and `lc_l` are `f64`, not `u32`.** The Exhibit 12-21 note says "Interpolate for noninteger values of right-side lateral clearance", which an integer field cannot express, and the previously dead interpolating lookup is now the implementation. Integer clearances read exactly the exhibit values, so existing results do not move; Python callers can pass ints or floats.

### Fixed

- **Base capacity was computed from the speed-adjusted free-flow speed.** The December 2022 corrections to the 7th Edition change Equations 12-6 and 12-7 from FFS_adj to FFS and state that "FFS used in the adjusted capacity computation is the original and unadjusted free-flow speed". `basicfreeways`, the Chapter 10 facilities engine, and the new Edition 7.1 modules all passed `ffs x saf`, so a speed adjustment factor suppressed capacity a second time on top of CAF. The breakpoint still uses FFS_adj; that asymmetry is deliberate. No published example problem could catch this because they all set SAF = 1.0. Results change wherever SAF varies: weather and incident scenarios, work zones, ATDM. Chapter 25 Example Problem 4 shifts as a result, documented in `docs/hcm/VERIFICATION.md`.
- **Minor-street left turns used the wrong Stage II conflicting movement.** The December 2022 corrections change Equations 20-14 and 20-15 and the matching Exhibit 20-16 rows so that a minor-street left turn's Stage II conflicting flow counts the opposing minor-street through movement, not the major-street right turn. The worked examples had always used the corrected form, which is why Chapter 32 Example Problems 3 and 4 previously reproduced only through explicit `conflicting_flow_overrides`. With the correction applied they reproduce natively, and those overrides are gone from the fixtures.
- **Minor-street crossing movements used pre-correction Exhibit 20-14 factors.** The same December 2022 corrections (page 20-18) swap the conflicting-movement-6 entries between movement 8 Stage II and movement 11 Stage I, so f(8,6) is the "channelized 0 / all others 1" form and f(11,6) the shared-lane 0.5 form. Example Problem 3's v_c,II,8 = 532 and v_c,I,11 = 482 now reproduce natively, and no TWSC fixture carries `conflicting_flow_overrides` any more. This entry was missed in the first pass over the corrections document and caught on re-review.
- **Eight-lane P_FM could go negative.** The Exhibit 14-8 regression `0.2178 - 0.000125 v_R` falls below zero past roughly 1,742 pc/h of ramp demand, putting a negative flow in Lanes 1 and 2 and a negative density downstream. Both eight-lane forms and the Exhibit 14-9 base form are now clamped to a proportion, with a VERIFY-HCM note that a clamp signals an input outside the regression's fitted range.
